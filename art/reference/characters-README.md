# Characters

Source art, not site content. Nothing in this folder has a public URL — Next
only serves `public/`. The holocron links each `*-reference-sheet.jpg` through
`/api/holocron/sheet/<name>`, which only answers a logged-in holocron session.
It lives in the repository so it is versioned, survives the machine it was made
on, and is on both machines after a pull.

All sheets are 1536 × 1024 JPEG. They arrive from the image model as 2 MB PNGs,
which is the wrong container for a rendered scene — converting costs nothing
visible and takes each one to about 300 KB.

## How these fit together

They are one family, but not one shape. There are three tiers, and which tier a
character sits in decides where its mark goes.

**Mascots — the mark is the head.** Gem and Clyde. A plump glowing symbol with
two large cartoon eyes on it, no face, on a chunky vinyl body about three heads
tall. The shape says which lab and so does the colour temperature: Gem is a
four-pointed star in cold blue-violet, Clyde a sunburst in warm amber-orange.

**People — the mark is worn.** Metis and DeekDeek. Same toy line, same big glossy
eyes, but a face instead of a symbol. The lab's mark moves onto the clothing,
where it is the only high-contrast thing on the figure. Their builds are their
own: she is lean, he is enormous.

**The pet — the mark is on a tag.** Chickadee. She is not a person at all, so
she wears it the way a pet does, on a small disc at the collar.

**Al is above all of them.** The catch-all, globe-headed, holding every colour
at once. He presides rather than participates, which is why arms-folded-watching
is his pose and would be wrong on any of the others.

Put them in one frame and it has a shape: mascots low and busy, people flanking,
Al centred and slightly back, looking at the camera while nobody else is.

## Gem

`gem-reference-sheet.jpg` — the recurring stand-in for Google's Gemini. Named as
a play on "Jim", so he reads as a bloke rather than a product.

**Fixed:** a small glossy 3D vinyl-toy figure about three heads tall, with a
plump four-pointed star for a head, glowing from within, electric blue at the
tips through to deep violet at the centre, and two large cartoon eyes.

**Wardrobe:** the black beanie with the star's top point poking through it, the
domino mask, the navy and pale-blue striped jersey, black gloves and boots, the
grey swag sack. He is dressed as a burglar because the first story was a
break-in. In a lab coat he is still Gem.

Four views, three expression heads — smug, alarmed, unconvincingly innocent —
and a tiptoe pose.

## Clyde

`clyde-reference-sheet.jpg` — the recurring stand-in for Anthropic's Claude.
Named the way Gem was: Claude is already a person's name, so it only needed one
letter to stop sounding like a product.

**Fixed:** the same vinyl build, cream matte body, with a plump sunburst for a
head, glowing from within, bright amber at the spoke tips through to deep orange
at the centre. The spokes are soft and slightly irregular rather than sharp or
geometric — he is a character, not a logo with legs.

**Wardrobe:** a white lab coat, from the first scene he appeared in.

Four views — the back view correctly has no eyes — three expression heads,
curious, delighted and thoughtful, and a listening pose.

**He does not go on a piece about somebody else's work.** A Claude-branded
character illustrating a breakthrough Anthropic had no part in implies something
untrue, and the accuracy rules do not stop at the prose.

## Metis

`metis-reference-sheet.jpg` — the recurring stand-in for Meta, and the only
woman in the lineup. Named for **Metis**, the Titan goddess of "good counsel,
planning, cunning and wisdom" ([Theoi](https://www.theoi.com/Titan/TitanisMetis.html)),
whom Zeus swallowed on a prophecy that she would bear a son greater than his
father. Athena was born from his head anyway. Nobody needs to know that for her
to read, and anyone who looks it up gets paid twice.

She was **Muta** until September 29, 2026, for Dea Muta, the Roman goddess of
silence. The meaning was right but the word was not: written out as a pilot's
name in Void Signal it looked rough, and a name has to survive being set in big
type. Metis keeps the Meta sound and the quiet — the game's line for her is
still "Says nothing. Misses nothing." The sheet was drawn while she was Muta;
nothing on it spells the name, so it stands.

**Fixed:** the same chunky toy build and the same large glossy cartoon eyes as
the mascots, but a face — a cute cartoon face with a smirk, under a big spiky
layered black bob with one teal streak. A zipped black leather jacket, black
trousers, black boots with a small heel. A glowing cyan infinity loop on the
jacket at her chest, which is the only light on her and the only colour.

**Punk rather than goth, and the distinction is the whole character.** Goth is
trying to look dangerous; she is not bothering to. No skulls, no stripes, no
purple, nothing sullen. She knows what everyone said and is not telling, and
that reads as ease rather than effort.

Four views, three expression heads — knowing, flatly unimpressed, and a wink —
and a pose with one hand on her hip and a finger raised to her lips.

## DeekDeek

`deekdeek-reference-sheet.jpg` — the recurring stand-in for DeepSeek, and the
biggest figure in the lineup. **Deep-Seek, said twice.** It is the most direct
name in the set — nothing is hidden, you hear the product straight off — and the
doubling makes him sound exactly as bright as he is.

He was built as Grok and reassigned. Grok went through Gronk, Greg and Mergen
without ever sounding like itself, and Mergen was the one that broke the rule
every other name keeps: Gem/Gemini, Clyde/Claude, Metis/Meta, Al/AI,
Chickadee/GPT all carry the product in the sound. Mergen only carried it in the
meaning, which nobody hears. DeepSeek had no character and a real claim on this
design, so he changed hands.

**Fixed:** a Mongolian bökh wrestler. Roughly three heads tall but nearly twice
the width of anyone else, broad chest, big round belly, thick legs, short thick
arms, small topknot. A cute cartoon face with the same big glossy eyes as the
rest and a wide open grin. Traditional bökh dress: a short open-fronted crimson
jacket with gold trim across the shoulders and back, dark trunks, turned-up
leather boots. A whale between the shoulders, which is the only high-contrast
thing on him.

**Bökh rather than sumo, deliberately.** Sumo is Japanese. Bökh is Mongolian
wrestling, practised in Inner Mongolia, and it solved three problems at once:
the same enormous silhouette, a jacket with a back to put the mark on, and boots
instead of bare feet, which read far better on a toy.

**He is formidable, never the butt of the joke.** Bökh wrestlers are huge and
dangerous, and he only works as both. The concentrating expression head carries
it — it is the one that stops the character being about his size.

Four views, three expression heads — delighted, surprised and deeply
concentrating — and the bökh eagle dance as his pose.

## Al

`al-reference-sheet.jpg` — the catch-all, for anything not about one particular
lab. **A.I. → Al**, the same joke a third time.

**Fixed:** the same vinyl build, warm grey matte body, with a large smooth
iridescent head — soap-bubble pearlescence shifting through gold, rose, lilac
and blue — and two large glossy cartoon eyes that stay readable against the
reflections. The head is deliberately larger than the others, because he is all
of them combined.

**An earlier version was a plain white orb and it failed twice**: a character
defined by absence has nothing to look at, and a featureless white figure with a
raised finger is the Michelin Man, which is exactly what the model drew. Chrome
would have reflected the room; iridescence holds every colour at once, Gem's
cold blue-violet and Clyde's warm amber included.

**Wardrobe:** a white lab coat, which has carried across scenes and turns out to
say "the labs" without a caption doing it.

Four views, three expression heads — attentive, amused and skeptical — and an
arms-folded pose. **The skeptical head is the one that earns him.** It can host
a piece about something going wrong, which the lab characters should not be asked
to do about themselves.

## Chickadee

`chickadee-reference-sheet.jpg` — the recurring stand-in for OpenAI, and the
only one who is not a person. She is the group's pet, and she is the smallest of
them, and in the podcast she is an old woman.

**The name arrived by accident and turned out to be the best one.** The choice
was between calling her Phoebe and calling her Chickadee — and the black-capped
chickadee has exactly two known vocalisations: the *chick-a-dee-dee-dee* call it
is named for, and the **fee-bee song**. The bird sings its own alternative name.
It is also a creature whose encyclopaedia entry is almost entirely about the
noises it makes, standing in for a language model.

**Fixed:** correct black-capped chickadee markings — glossy black cap, black bib,
bright white cheeks, white belly, warm buff sides, soft grey wings, back and
tail. Small round wire spectacles on the beak. A slim collar with a plain pale
metal disc hanging at the throat. Roughly two-thirds the height of the others.

**She is an old woman, not a baby bird**, and the spectacles do the work: the
expression head looking over the top of them is the whole character. Composed,
upright, wings folded behind her back like hands, dry and entirely unimpressed.

Four views, three expression heads — dry amusement, sharp disapproval over the
spectacles, and delight — and a pose standing very still with her head tilted,
mid-listen.

**The tag is deliberately blank.** Image models render a logo as mush, so the
disc comes back clean and what goes on it is decided later.

## Scofti, and Zyra

The two humans in the set, and neither stands in for a lab. **Scofti** is the
one real person — Michael. **Zyra** is his made-up human — the secret weapon.
Same toy line and the same big glossy eyes as everyone else, but each
has **a sheet for peace and one for war** (Zyra also has an F1 suit), and a scene puts the pair in
the same mode unless that mismatch is the joke. Always attach the sheet;
describing either of them again from scratch gets a different person.

**Scofti** — `scofti-peace-reference-sheet.jpg`, `scofti-war-reference-sheet.jpg`.
**Fixed:** brown buzzed hair, a five o'clock shadow that is always there, a
black watch on the left wrist and a black paracord bracelet on each wrist.
**Peace:** backwards olive cap, black sunglasses, black t-shirt, blue-and-black
camo cargo pants, olive suede Pumas. **War:** the same, plus an olive plate
carrier with a radio on the chest and sergeant's chevrons on the cap. Both
sheets have four views, a hands-in-pockets pose, and three heads — sunglasses
on, sunglasses up, worried. The worried head is the one Clyde consoles.

**Zyra** — `zyra-war-reference-sheet.jpg`, `zyra-peace-reference-sheet.jpg`,
`zyra-f1-reference-sheet.jpg`.
**Fixed:** long wavy dark-brown hair in a high ponytail, brown eyes, a smirk.
**War:** olive tactical jacket with sleeves pushed up over a black top, chest
rig with pouches, fingerless gloves, thigh holster, olive cargo pants, black
combat boots, a winged patch on the shoulder. **Peace:** a sleeveless floral
mini dress in orange, pink and yellow, gold hoop earrings, white go-go boots.
Both have four views, three heads — smirk, focused, laughing — and a pose with
one boot up on a supply crate. The war sheet was made first and the peace one
from it, which is why the face and the pose match.

**F1:** a Scofti racing suit — black top with SCOFTI across the chest,
lime-green legs, black-and-lime flames down both, black-and-lime gloves and
boots, a matching helmet with a gold visor. Same face, same three heads, posed
with one boot up on a stack of tyres in a white hangar. It was picked over a
white-camo suit and a daisy-liveried one, made the same way and not kept.

The blonde in the /miracle concert picture is Zyra before the sheets existed.
Her look was chosen for colour there; the sheets are what she is now.

## Still to come

**xAI's Grok has nobody.** Three names were tried and discarded — Gronk sounded
like a footballer, Greg stopped fitting once he was no longer Indian, and Mergen
did not sound like Grok at all. The design he was wearing went to DeepSeek, so
Grok starts again from nothing whenever somebody wants it.

## To make a new scene

Upload the character's sheet with the prompt and say "this exact character, new
scene." Re-describing from scratch drifts. For a group scene, upload every sheet
involved.

Render style is the site's: bright, bold, futuristic, rendered 3D in a bright
architectural space, never flat vector and never floating on empty white.

Entries carrying one of these images use `caption: "Illustration."` A company
drawn as a burglar is plainly satire, and the caption is what stops it being read
as documentation of an event. The `alt` describes what is literally in the frame.

## Scenes

`scenes/` holds finished group pictures — the ones worth keeping rather than
every render. They are reference too: when a new scene needs the six together,
one of these is a faster starting point than the six sheets and a paragraph.

`the-podcast.jpg` — 1774 × 887. All six around a studio table with microphones
and headphones. Chickadee is standing on a stack of books to reach hers,
DeekDeek is spilling over two seats, Clyde has his notes out, Metis has her arms
folded and is saying nothing, and Al is centre looking down the lens.

`the-portrait.jpg` — 1536 × 1024. The formal lineup, everyone posed, Chickadee
on a plinth at the front.

`the-couch.jpg` — 1536 × 1024. All six crowded onto one couch, DeekDeek having
taken the middle, Gem half off one end, Chickadee perched on the back.

`the-arcade.jpg` — 1536 × 1024. All six in a neon arcade: Metis and Clyde on the
sticks, DeekDeek cheering, Chickadee on a stool, Gem leaning over a cabinet.
On `/arcade`.

`the-tanks.jpg` — 1536 × 1024. All six in an aisle of lit aquariums, everyone
but Al and Chickadee pointing at the fish. On `/tanks`.

**One rule holds all three together: Al is the only one who looks at the
camera.** It is what makes him read as presiding rather than just standing
there, and it gives every group shot the same spine.

Six characters in one frame is where a model starts dropping people and
inventing extras. Count heads before accepting a render.

How it plays

As real as we can make it, in our style. You race from the back of an 8-car field, and the car behaves like a stock car: grip runs out, tires wear, and the draft matters.

Controls

Keyboard
← → steer · ↑ gas · ↓ brake · C changes the view · P calls a pit stop · Space pauses
Phone
Steering pad bottom left, gas and brake pedals bottom right
Gamepad
Left stick steers · right trigger or A for gas · left trigger or B to brake
Views
Right behind the car, or in the seat looking over the dash
Exit
The red ✕ EXIT button is always on screen and goes straight back to the garage

Two ways to race

Preseason testing
The default. Walls and bumps slow you down but don't wreck you, and a car knocked sideways straightens itself. There's no brake pedal: let off the gas and the car slows itself for the corner. A little extra grip, and the rivals are slower and gentler.
Race night
The real thing. Brake yourself, hit the wall and you can spin, and the rivals race hard.

The race

Start
Pace laps double file behind the pace car, then the green at the line
Length
15, 30 or 60 laps
Grip
Brake and turn share the same grip, so braking hard while turning slides you up the track. The banking in the turns adds grip.
Draft
Tuck in behind a car on the straights and you pick up speed
Contact
Rubbin' is racin'. A hard hit off-center can turn a car around (race night only).

Tires

Wear
All four wear on their own. Martinsvale is all left turns, so the right side wears about twice as fast. Braking works the fronts, the gas works the rears, and sliding chews all four.
Cost
A set loses roughly 5% a lap driven hard. Worn tires cost about a second a lap.
Heat
Cold tires grip less: take it easy for a lap after a stop. Overheated tires show orange.
Monitor
The TIRES box shows each tire's % left and °F. The crew chief warns you at half and when they're about gone.

Pit stops

Call it
Press P or tap PIT. Your car drives itself down pit road on the 35 mph limiter.
Air gun
Four fresh tires. The crew alone takes 16 s, a second less per pit crew upgrade. Every tap on the air gun takes time off; hammer it and you're out in about 7½ s.
Rivals
They pit when their tires are going, and they answer your stop over the next few laps so nobody is stuck on old tires

Plan: Scofti tokens

Not built yet. When the time comes, upgrades are bought with tokens that players buy. The business accounts and D-U-N-S numbers already exist; this is what has to be in place first, in order.

  1. Player accounts and a server wallet. Cash and upgrades live on the device today, which anyone can edit. Bought tokens have to live on our server, tied to a Scofti arcade sign-in, with a ledger of every purchase and every spend. One wallet for the whole arcade, so tokens work in every Scofti game.
  2. Prices in tokens. Each upgrade gets a token price next to (or instead of) its cash price. Decide whether race cash stays as a second, earned currency. Recommendation: keep it, so a player who never pays can still progress, just slower.
  3. Checkout on the web. Stripe Checkout under the business account, in test mode first. A Stripe webhook credits the tokens on our server; the game only reads the balance and never adds tokens itself.
  4. Token packs. A few fixed packs with clear prices, no random rewards. Suggested starting point: 100 / 550 / 1,200 tokens, with a small bonus on the bigger packs.
  5. Kids and parents. The players are kids, so purchases sit behind a parent check, with a spending cap and receipts emailed to the parent. Under-13 sign-ups need a parent's consent (COPPA) and no ad tracking.
  6. App stores, later. If the game ships as a phone app, Apple and Google require their own in-app purchase for digital items. The D-U-N-S number is what enrolls the business as the developer on both. The server wallet stays the same; their receipts get checked on our server before tokens are credited.
  7. Turn it on. Test the whole flow with test cards, refunds and a parent saying no, then switch Stripe to live.
